Trying to research the ethics of every single purchase leads to exhaustion, not better decisions. A simpler framework holds up better over time.

## Buy less, first

The single most reliably "ethical" purchase is the one you don't make. Before researching a brand's supply chain, ask whether you need the item at all — or whether something you already own solves the same problem.

<Callout type="tip">
  Set a 48-hour rule for non-essential purchases. Most of the guilt around
  "unethical" shopping comes from impulse buys you didn't need in the first
  place.
</Callout>

## When you do need to buy

- Prioritize durability over trend — a well-made item worn for years beats a "sustainable" item replaced every season
- Buy secondhand where reasonable — it's often the most sustainable option available
- Pick two or three causes you care most about (labor conditions, materials, packaging) rather than trying to optimize for everything at once

Perfect ethical consumption doesn't exist. A realistic framework you'll actually follow beats an ideal one you'll abandon after a month.
