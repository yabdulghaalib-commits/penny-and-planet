The average household throws away close to a fifth of the food it buys. That's not just an environmental problem — it's money going straight into the trash.

## Start with a "eat this first" shelf

Designate one shelf in your fridge for anything close to its use-by date. Nothing fancy — just a consistent spot you check before reaching for something new.

<Callout type="tip">
  Put this shelf at eye level. Food that's out of sight gets forgotten, and
  forgotten food gets wasted.
</Callout>

## Plan meals around what you already have

Before writing a grocery list, do a five-minute inventory of your fridge and pantry. Build two or three meals around what's already there, then shop to fill the gaps.

- Check the fridge, freezer, and pantry first
- Plan meals that share overlapping ingredients
- Buy only what those meals actually need

## Freeze more than you think you can

Bread, milk, fresh herbs, and even cooked rice all freeze better than most people expect. If something is about to turn, freezing it is almost always better than tossing it.

<Callout type="note" title="Not sure if it's still good?">
  "Best by" dates are about peak quality, not safety. Use your senses first —
  smell, texture, appearance — before assuming something needs to go.
</Callout>

Small habits like these compound fast. A household that cuts food waste in half often sees a noticeable drop in their grocery bill within a single month.
