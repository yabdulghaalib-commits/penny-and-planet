Cost-per-wear is a simple idea: divide what you paid for an item by how many times you actually wear it. It reframes "expensive" and "cheap" in a way sticker price alone can't.

## The math that changes everything

A $120 coat worn 200 times costs $0.60 per wear. A $20 top worn twice costs $10 per wear. Price alone tells you almost nothing about value.

<Callout type="note">
  Cost-per-wear rewards buying fewer, better things you'll actually reach
  for — not necessarily buying expensive things.
</Callout>

## Building toward a smaller, more-worn wardrobe

- Track what you actually wear for one month before buying anything new
- Notice the pieces you reach for repeatedly — that's your real style, not your aspirational one
- Buy replacements only for genuinely worn-out favorites
- Choose versatile neutrals that mix with more of what you own

A smaller wardrobe built this way tends to cost less overall, create less waste, and — somewhat counterintuitively — make getting dressed easier.
