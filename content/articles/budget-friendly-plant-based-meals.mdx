Plant-based eating has a reputation for being expensive — usually because of pre-made "alternative" products. Built around whole ingredients, it's often one of the cheapest ways to eat well.

## The cheap, filling core

Beans, lentils, rice, and seasonal vegetables form the backbone of some of the least expensive meals per serving available at any grocery store, plant-based or not.

<Callout type="tip">
  Buy dried beans and lentils instead of canned when you can. They cost a
  fraction as much per serving and keep almost indefinitely in the pantry.
</Callout>

## A simple weekly rotation

1. A big pot of lentil or bean soup, portioned for several days
2. A grain bowl built around whatever vegetables are cheapest that week
3. One "effort" meal on the weekend when you have more time to cook

<Callout type="note">
  Skip the pre-made meat alternatives if budget is the priority — they're
  often priced closer to meat than to the whole ingredients they're made
  from.
</Callout>

Plant-based eating and frugal eating overlap more than most people expect — the cheapest ingredients in most grocery stores tend to be plants.
