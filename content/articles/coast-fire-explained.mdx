Somewhere in most financial independence conversations, someone mentions retiring at 35 on a 70% savings rate, and the whole idea starts to sound like it's only for people willing to eat rice and beans for a decade. Coast FIRE is the version of financial independence that doesn't require that.

## What "coasting" actually means

The idea: once the money already invested is on track to grow, on its own, into what's needed by retirement age, no further contributions are required to that account. You've reached "coast." From there, income only needs to cover current expenses. Retirement is already being handled by compound growth running quietly in the background.

Here's what that can look like with real numbers. Say someone is 30 with $150,000 already invested, and stops contributing entirely from that point on. Assuming a long-run average return of around 7% a year, a common planning assumption for a diversified stock portfolio and not a guarantee, that account alone could grow to somewhere around $1.6 million by 65, with no further contributions at all. That's the whole mechanism. The earlier the number gets large enough, the more decades compound growth has to do the rest of the work.

<Callout type="note">
  Coast FIRE isn't about retiring early. It's about removing the pressure to keep saving aggressively for retirement once existing investments are already on track to get there.
</Callout>

## Why it appeals to people who don't want the extreme version

- It lowers financial anxiety without demanding an extreme savings rate for years on end
- It opens the door to lower-paying, lower-stress work, since income only needs to cover today's expenses
- It rewards starting early far more than it rewards saving aggressively later, since time in the market is doing most of the work

## The honest tradeoff

The math only works with enough runway left. Someone hitting "coast" status at 30 has 30-plus years for growth to compound. Someone trying the same move at 50 has far less time for the math to work, and likely needs a much larger number already invested before coasting makes sense at all. This is a strategy that rewards an early start more than almost anything else in personal finance, and it's a weak rescue plan late in a career.

## Is it actually worth aiming for?

For someone who already dislikes their high-earning, high-stress job and would rather trade income for time, yes, deliberately. For someone who genuinely likes their career and isn't in a hurry to change anything, Coast FIRE is less a goal to chase and more a fact that becomes true one day, almost by accident, if the savings rate was reasonable in the years it mattered most.
