Most budgets fail for the same reason: they assume a version of you with more discipline and fewer surprises than real life actually has.

## Give every dollar a job — including "unplanned"

Zero-based budgeting means every dollar of income is assigned a category before the month starts, including savings and debt payoff. The trick that makes it survivable: include a "miscellaneous / unplanned" category every month, funded on purpose.

<Callout type="tip">
  Budget for surprises on purpose. An unplanned-expenses category isn't a
  failure of planning — it's the plan.
</Callout>

## A simple monthly structure

1. List all expected income
2. Assign fixed essentials first (rent, utilities, minimum debt payments)
3. Assign savings and debt payoff goals next — treat them like a bill
4. Assign flexible categories (groceries, fun money) with realistic amounts
5. Leave 5–10% unassigned as a buffer

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistake">
  Budgeting your "ideal" grocery spending instead of your actual historical
  average is the fastest way to abandon a budget within a month.
</Callout>
