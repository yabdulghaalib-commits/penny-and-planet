The average budget doesn't fail in month three. It fails in week two, usually right after an expense nobody planned for shows up and blows a hole in a category that was already tight. The problem usually isn't the person. It's that most budgets are built for a version of life with no surprises in it, and that version of life doesn't exist.

Zero-based budgeting fixes a lot of that, but only if there's room built in for reality from the start.

## The core idea

Every dollar of income gets assigned somewhere before the month begins: rent, groceries, debt payments, savings, all of it. Nothing sits around unassigned. The name comes from the goal of ending the month at zero *unassigned* dollars, not zero dollars total.

<Callout type="tip">
  Budget for surprises on purpose. An "unplanned expenses" category funded every single month isn't a failure of planning. It's the plan working correctly.
</Callout>

## Building your first month

1. **List all expected income.** If it varies, use your lowest realistic estimate from the past few months, not your best month.
2. **Assign fixed essentials first**: rent or mortgage, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments. These get paid regardless of what else happens.
3. **Treat savings and debt payoff like a bill.** If it isn't assigned before the flexible categories, it usually doesn't happen.
4. **Assign flexible categories realistically**, based on what you actually spent last month, not what you wish you'd spent.
5. **Leave 5 to 10% genuinely unassigned** as a buffer for the expense nobody saw coming.

<Callout type="warning" title="The mistake that sinks most budgets">
  Budgeting your ideal grocery spending instead of your actual historical average is the fastest way to abandon a budget within a month. If you've been spending $650 a month on groceries, don't budget $400 because it sounds more responsible. Budget $650, then work on lowering it gradually.
</Callout>

## What "sticking to it" actually looks like

A budget that survives real life isn't one that never needs adjusting. It's one that's allowed to be adjusted without starting over. If the unplanned category runs out by the 20th because the car needed a repair, that's the system working, not failing. Move money between categories, note what happened, and keep going.

The goal was never a perfect month. It was knowing where the money is supposed to go before it's already gone.
