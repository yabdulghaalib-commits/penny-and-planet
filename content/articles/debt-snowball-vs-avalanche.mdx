Two popular methods dominate debt payoff advice, and every personal finance article seems to insist there's one correct answer. There isn't, quite. There's a mathematically optimal one and a psychologically effective one, and they're not always the same method.

## The two methods, side by side

| Method | Order of payoff | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Snowball | Smallest balance first | Needing quick, motivating wins |
| Avalanche | Highest interest rate first | Minimizing total interest paid |

Snowball pays off the smallest balance first, regardless of interest rate, then rolls that payment into the next-smallest balance. Avalanche targets the highest interest rate first, regardless of size, because that's the debt costing the most money every month it sticks around.

## What the difference actually looks like in dollars

Take someone with three debts: an $800 store card at 8%, a $4,000 credit card at 24%, and a $9,000 car loan at 6%, putting $250 a month toward extra payments on top of the minimums. Working the numbers out: avalanche finishes about a month faster and saves roughly $170 in interest compared to snowball, in this particular mix of balances and rates.

<Callout type="tip">
  That gap is smaller than most debt-payoff content implies. Avalanche is almost always the cheaper method, but the size of the savings depends heavily on how spread out your interest rates actually are. A single high-rate credit card sitting next to mostly low-rate debt makes a bigger difference than three cards all sitting around 20%.
</Callout>

## How to actually choose

If you've started and abandoned a debt payoff plan before, the snowball's early win, an entire balance gone in a month or two, is often worth more than the extra interest it costs. Motivation is not a minor factor in whether a plan gets finished.

If you're not especially tempted to quit and the numbers genuinely bother you, avalanche is the better call. It's not always a large difference, as the example above shows, but it's never worse, and sometimes the gap is much bigger than $170, especially with several high-rate cards involved.

There's also a hybrid option worth knowing about: pay off whichever debt is both small and high-rate first, if one exists, then switch to strict avalanche order for the rest. That gets an early motivating win without giving up much on interest.

Either way, the biggest lever isn't the order. It's the total amount going toward debt each month. Automate the minimums on everything else, pick a method, and put every spare dollar toward the target debt without renegotiating the plan every few weeks.
