Grocery bills creep up slowly enough that most people don't notice until they're suddenly spending $200 more a month than they did a year ago. Fixing that doesn't require clipping coupons for an hour every Sunday. It mostly comes down to a handful of habits, done consistently.

## Where the real savings are

1. **Shop with a list, and actually stick to it.** Impulse items are where budgets quietly bleed out. A list turns "maybe I'll grab that" into a decision you already made at home, when you weren't hungry and had time to think it through.
2. **Compare unit price, not the price on the shelf tag.** The bigger box isn't always cheaper per ounce. Most stores print unit price in small text below the sticker price. It's worth a glance before assuming size equals value.
3. **Buy pantry staples in bulk, produce in small batches.** Rice, oats, and dried beans keep for months and get cheaper per pound the more you buy. Fresh produce goes bad in days, so buying it in bulk usually just means throwing more of it away later.
4. **Build a rotation of 8 to 10 meals you actually enjoy cooking.** This does more for a grocery bill than almost anything else on this list, because it turns shopping from an open-ended guessing game into a short, repeatable list.
5. **Check your pantry before you check the store.** Half-used bags of pasta and forgotten cans of beans are often enough for a meal you'd otherwise pay for again.
6. **Choose store brands for staples.** For flour, oats, canned tomatoes, and spices, the difference between store brand and name brand is usually the label, not the product. Save brand loyalty for the handful of items where you can genuinely taste a difference.
7. **Batch-cook once a week.** An hour on a Sunday making a big pot of something can replace three or four nights of $15 takeout. That's the trade that actually moves a budget.
8. **Keep a rough memory of what things cost.** No spreadsheet required, just a general sense of "eggs are usually around this much here" so it's obvious when something's overpriced, or genuinely on sale.
9. **Don't shop hungry.** It's a cliché precisely because it keeps being true. Eat something first, or shop right after a meal instead of before one.
10. **Track your spending for one month before changing anything else.** It's hard to fix what you haven't actually looked at, and most people are surprised by where the money goes once they check.

<Callout type="tip" title="Pick two, not ten">
  Trying to overhaul all ten habits in the same week rarely sticks. Choose the two that feel easiest for your life right now, run them for a month, then add another.
</Callout>

## The one habit that does the most work

If only one thing on this list gets adopted, make it meal planning around what's already in the kitchen. It does two jobs at once: it stops food from going bad before it gets used, and it stops new purchases that duplicate what's already sitting in the pantry. Everything else here saves a little. This one tends to save the most, for the least ongoing effort.
