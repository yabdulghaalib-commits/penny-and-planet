You don't need a restrictive diet or hours of coupon-clipping to meaningfully cut your grocery bill. A handful of consistent habits do most of the work.

## The list

1. Shop with a list — and stick to it
2. Compare unit price, not sticker price
3. Buy pantry staples in bulk, produce in small batches
4. Build a rotation of 8–10 cheap, filling meals you actually enjoy
5. Shop your pantry before adding anything to the cart
6. Choose store brands for staples — the quality gap is usually small
7. Batch-cook once a week to avoid expensive last-minute takeout
8. Keep a running price memory for the items you buy most
9. Avoid shopping hungry — it's a cliché because it's true
10. Track your grocery spending for one month before making bigger changes

<Callout type="tip" title="Start with just two">
  Trying to change all ten habits at once rarely sticks. Pick two, run them
  for a month, then add another.
</Callout>

## The habit that saves the most

Of everything on this list, meal planning around what you already have tends to save the most money for the least effort — it prevents both food waste and impulse purchases in one move.
